# Identification feasibility

Issue #7. This file assesses whether the panel from issue #6 supports a causal estimate of the
insurance effect on home values (E2 and E3 in `docs/estimands.md`). The numbers come from
`output/donor_diagnostics.md` and `output/donor_pretrends.png`, generated by
`uv run python -m analysis.donors` from `data/panel_zip_year.csv` and `data/event_register.csv`
only. If the inputs change, rerun the script and update the numbers here.

## 1. Design inputs

- **Treated units.** The six core ZCTAs of `docs/schema.md`. Five have ZHVI for the full fit
  period (95945, 95946, 95949, 95959, 95975). 95986 has no ZHVI. The three fringe ZCTAs
  (95602, 95960, 95977) are a sensitivity set (rule X5 of `docs/wildfire_timeline.md`).
- **Pre-period.** It ends in 2016, the year before the first register event that lists a
  target ZCTA (`FIRE_2017_LOBO`, October 2017). The code derives this year from the register,
  not from outcomes. Fit years are 2001-2016 (16 years; ZHVI for the core ZCTAs starts in 2001).
- **Weights.** Synthetic-control weights (non-negative, sum to 1) fitted on unit-demeaned log
  real ZHVI for 2001-2016. `select_weights` raises an error if it gets a later year, and
  `tests/test_donors.py` records the columns passed to the optimizer and checks that the weights
  do not change when post-2016 prices change.
- **Donor rules.** These rules are set before any outcome comparison: no moratorium listing,
  no register fire damage, 2018 S2 high/extreme risk share below 0.25, not adjacent (proxy),
  complete ZHVI. `output/donor_diagnostics.md` section 9 gives each of the 239 non-target ZCTAs
  a decision and a reason.
- **Bay Area.** The 180 Bay Area demand-linkage ZCTAs (`panel_role = spillover_source`) are
  never in a pool. Their price index is reported only as a spillover measure (section 4).

## 2. Donor pools and contamination

| pool | rules | donors | hold-out RMSPE | placebo median | max pre gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A strict (main) | all | 11 | 0.040 | 0.022 | 0.151 |
| B hazard relaxed | no hazard rule | 14 | 0.116 | 0.035 | 0.127 |
| C contamination relaxed | moratorium and fire rules dropped | 12 | 0.077 | 0.023 | 0.139 |
| D adjacency relaxed | adjacency rule dropped | 11 (same as A) | 0.040 | 0.022 | 0.151 |

- **Pool A** has 11 ZCTAs, all with main county Placer (06061): mostly the Roseville, Rocklin,
  Granite Bay and Loomis suburbs. These are lower-hazard, higher-income suburban markets
  (section 3). They are not rural foothill markets like the core ZCTAs.
- **Insurance change in donors is not measured.** The panel has S2, S3 and S6 ZIP insurance
  values only for target ZCTAs (missing code 5 elsewhere, `docs/panel_audit.md` section 2).
  All 59 non-Bay-Area candidates, and all 11 pool A donors, have insurance status `unknown`. No
  donor can be certified "low insurance increase". The only measured insurance-side exposure for
  donors is a moratorium listing in the register, and the contamination rule uses it. For scale,
  every target ZCTA had a measured W2 increase: nonrenewal rate +3 to +12 percentage points,
  real HO premium +5% to +23% in log terms.
- **Contamination test.** Pool C adds moratorium-listed or burned ZCTAs. Its pre-period
  hold-out error is about twice that of pool A, and its synthetic puts 0.78 of the weight on
  96142 (Tahoma, listed on the 2021 Caldor moratoria). The contaminated pool does not fit better,
  so contamination does not buy pre-period fit.
- **Spillover test.** Without ZCTA geometry in the allowed inputs, adjacency is a proxy: same
  county as a core ZCTA, or co-listed with a core ZCTA on a register event. Thirty candidates
  are adjacency-proxy ZCTAs. Each one also fails another rule, so pool D equals pool A. The data
  cannot test spillover separately from contamination.

## 3. Pre-period fit, balance and pretrends

- **Fit.** For pool A, the in-sample fit RMSPE is 0.061 log points. The pre-period hold-out
  (weights fitted on 2001-2011, error on 2012-2016) is 0.040. That ranks 9 of 12 against
  placebo-in-space fits of the donors themselves, so the treated series is fitted worse than a
  typical donor. Two donors carry the weight: 95663 (0.895) and 95746 (0.105).
- **Shape.** The treated-minus-pool gap is not linear. It runs from about -0.11 in 2004-2005 to
  +0.15 in 2010 and back near 0 by 2013 (right panel of `output/donor_pretrends.png`). Foothill
  prices fell later and less than suburban prices in the 2007-2011 bust, then recovered more
  slowly. A comparison that diverges by 0.15 log points during a pre-period cycle cannot isolate
  a post-2019 effect smaller than that.
- **Pretrend test.** The OLS slope of treated minus pool-A mean is 0.0094 log points per year
  (SE 0.0040, HAC p = 0.02) over 16 years. The minimum detectable trend (80% power, 5% test)
  is 0.011 log points per year, or about 0.06 over the five-year W2 post-period. The test
  rejects parallel linear trends. A test that did not reject would still be weak: it could miss
  a trend of 0.011 per year with 20% probability. Pools B and C give the same result (slopes
  0.009-0.010, p <= 0.01).
- **Balance.** On the structural covariates, the synthetic is far from the treated mean. The
  2018 S2 high/extreme share is 0.63 treated vs 0.07 synthetic (std diff 1.9). ACS 2017-2021
  real median income gives std diff -2.0, and vacancy gives 1.5. Low hazard is a donor rule, so
  a hazard imbalance is unavoidable within the region: no low-hazard ZCTA is a structural match
  for a high-hazard foothill ZCTA. 35 of the 56 region ZCTAs fail the hazard rule (34 at or
  above 0.25, 1 with no 2018 score).

## 4. Bay Area demand linkage

The Bay Area index uses 161 ZCTAs with complete 2001-2016 ZHVI. Over the fit years, the treated
mean has a correlation of 0.88 with that index in annual log growth. The pool A synthetic has a
correlation of 0.92. Both comparison and treated move with Bay Area demand in the pre-period. A
post-2020 remote-work shock could still affect them differently: rural foothills with
amenities and suburban commuter ZCTAs had different exposure to it. This is reported as a
spillover measure. It does not make Bay Area ZCTAs eligible as controls, and many of them had
their own fire and insurance exposure (`docs/estimands.md` section 5).

## 5. Event-study and difference-in-differences feasibility

- **Staggered timing.** From the register, the first core moratorium is in 2020 for 95945,
  95946, 95959, 95975 and 95986, and in 2021 for 95949. The first fire damage is in 2017 for
  95949, 95959 and 95975, and in 2021 for 95945. W2 (2019) and W3 (2023) are common to all ZCTAs.
  With only two adoption years and five priced units, a heterogeneity-robust staggered estimator
  (Callaway-Sant'Anna, Sun-Abraham) has one cohort of four units and one cohort of one unit.
  The never-treated group is pool A, which is not a valid comparison (sections 2-3).
- **Treatment and fire are not separable.** Every core moratorium year has a register fire in
  a core ZCTA in the same or the prior year (0 of 6 core ZCTAs pass this check). A moratorium
  event study measures the local fire plus the moratorium (`docs/wildfire_timeline.md`
  section 7).
- **Pretrends.** Price pre-periods are long (19-20 years before W1b), but the pretrend test
  rejects linear parallel trends, and the pre-period gap is cyclical (section 3). For insurance
  outcomes, S3 gives four pre-years (2015-2018) and S2 gives one (2018), only for target ZCTAs.
  An insurance pretrend test against donors is not possible.
- **ACS overlapping windows.** The panel has the windows 2017-2021, 2018-2022, 2019-2023 and
  2020-2024. Each window includes at least three years from 2019 or later, and no window lies
  wholly before 2019. Adjacent windows share four of five years, so successive values are not
  independent observations. ACS covariates can serve only as descriptive balance checks, and
  the report marks them as overlapping the post-period. They cannot give a pre-period or an
  annual population series.
- **Clusters.** The main design has 16 ZCTA clusters (5 treated, 11 donors) in 2 counties (1
  treated county). This is under the threshold of about 20, so cluster-robust standard errors
  are not reliable. The wild cluster bootstrap is also unreliable with one treated county and
  five treated ZCTAs. Use randomization (placebo-in-space) inference over the donor pool, and
  Conley-Taber intervals for few treated groups, instead. With 11 donors, the smallest
  attainable placebo p-value is 1/12 = 0.083, so no 5% test is possible.

## 6. Synthetic-control feasibility

Synthetic control is the better-suited design for five treated ZCTAs with a long price
pre-period. It fails here:

1. The donors' own insurance changes are unknown. Low insurance exposure, the defining
   property of a control, cannot be verified for any donor.
2. The low-hazard donors are structurally different (balance std diffs of 1.0-2.0). The
   treated series also falls outside the donors' range: the pre-period hold-out fit ranks 9 of 12.
3. The pool is small and lies in one county. The randomization inference floor of 0.083 is
   too high for a 5% test.
4. If the hazard rule is relaxed (pool B), the fit gets worse (hold-out 0.116, 14 of 15), so
   more donors do not help.

## 7. Verdict

**Causal identification is rejected.** No E2 or E3 estimate may be reported as causal from
this panel. The reasons, in order of weight:

1. Donor insurance change is not measured. The "low insurance increase" property of the
   control group cannot be established, and the result is `unknown` for all 59 candidates.
2. In the target, insurance treatment is not separable from local fire destruction. Every
   moratorium follows a local fire within a year.
3. The comparison is poor. Within the region, the low-hazard donors are structurally unlike
   the target. The pretrend test rejects parallel trends (p = 0.02, MDT 0.011 per year), and
   the pre-period gap reaches 0.15 log points.
4. There are 16 clusters in 2 counties. Cluster-robust inference is not reliable, and the
   permutation inference floor is 0.083.

### What would change the verdict

The verdict is conditional on the inputs, not permanent. Identification would be worth
re-assessing if all of these become true:

- ZIP insurance data (S2 premium, S3 nonrenewals) for candidate donor ZCTAs, so that their
  insurance change can be measured.
- A wider donor universe beyond the region (for example, other Sierra foothill counties). It
  needs the same wildfire and moratorium flags (rule X4), so that high-hazard donors with low
  insurance change can be found.
- At least about 20 clusters, or a design with a credible permutation distribution of a
  similar size.
- Variation in insurance that is not tied to a local fire, for example moratorium listings
  caused by fires elsewhere (`docs/wildfire_timeline.md` section 7).

## 8. Descriptive fallback

Report the following, with descriptive labels only:

1. **Event-time series by window.** Plot the treated core ZCTAs' log real ZHVI and the
   measured insurance series (S2 premium, S3 nonrenewal rate, S6 FAIR share) in event time for
   W1a, W1b, W2 and W3 separately. Apply exclusions X1 (destruction years) and X3 (moratorium
   years), and show results with and without the fringe ZCTAs.
2. **Benchmarks, not controls.** Show the pool A synthetic and the pool A mean, with the
   pre-period weights from `analysis/donors.py` frozen, as a benchmark line. Always show the
   2001-2016 pre-period gap next to any post-period gap, and the placebo band from the donors.
   Do not call the post-period difference an effect.
3. **Associations.** Report E1 (the population association conditional on insurance) at
   county level, with and without insurance controls, as `docs/estimands.md` allows.
4. **Wording.** Use "moved with", "coincided with" and "compared with a low-hazard benchmark".
   Do not use "caused", "effect of insurance" or "insurance reduced prices by".
5. **Uncertainty.** Show ranges from the placebo band and the fringe sensitivity, not
   cluster-robust confidence intervals.
