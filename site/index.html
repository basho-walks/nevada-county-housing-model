Nevada County, California · Housing model

When more people move to Nevada County, what happens to home prices in Grass Valley and Nevada City?

This model uses 24 years of data from California counties to measure how population growth changes home values. It then applies that result to the two cities. All dollar values are in dollars, adjusted for inflation.

25 years of population and prices

Population grew slowly and steadily. Home values swung much more, with the 2000s housing bubble, the 2008 crash and the pandemic jump in 2020-2021. Population growth is one cause among several, which is why the model needs data from many counties to measure it.

Prices keep rising for a few years after people arrive

Home prices have momentum. A price rise this year tends to bring more rise next year. So a one-time 1% increase in population lifts prices a little at first, then more over the next three to four years, until the effect levels off.

How the model works

  1. Population effect across California. County effects remove fixed local differences. Year effects remove things that hit every county at once, such as mortgage rates and recessions.
  2. County to city. Each city's Zillow Home Value Index is compared with the Nevada County price index for 2002-.
  3. Scenarios. A constant yearly population growth rate runs through the fitted model for years. The shaded range shows 90% of 10,000 simulations that vary the estimated coefficients.

Two estimates, and why they differ

Population and prices affect each other. People move toward jobs and amenities, and high prices push some people away. The same-year estimate uses this year's population growth and can overstate the effect. The lagged estimate uses last year's population growth, which this year's prices cannot have caused. It is the cautious estimate. The real effect is probably between the two.

Limits

  • The model treats a population decline as the mirror image of growth. In practice, declines often hit prices harder, because existing homes do not go away.
  • Census has no corrected 2010-2020 county population series. The model spreads the gap between the 2010s estimates and the 2020 Census over 2011-2019.
  • Zillow city values cover all home types at the middle of the market. Individual neighborhoods and home types can move differently.

Sources

  • Home prices: FHFA all-transactions house price index by county, and the Zillow Home Value Index for each city.
  • Population: U.S. Census Bureau county population estimates.
  • Income and building permits: Bureau of Economic Analysis and Census Bureau, through FRED.
  • Inflation: Consumer Price Index for all urban consumers (CPI-U).
